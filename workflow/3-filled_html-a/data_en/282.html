For more information, see the back of this form. (Attach separate sheet(s) if necessary.)
The workers were engaged and treated as independent contractors: paid on a per-project basis, provided invoices, and paid no vacation pay. In practice the company set working hours, provided standard tools and materials, directly supervised day-to-day tasks, and maintained exclusive control over contract terms. Workers were required to follow company policies and could not subcontract the work. We contend the Canada Revenue Agency and/or Service Canada incorrectly classified these workers as employees for CPP/EI purposes. We are appealing the assessed CPP contributions, EI premiums, penalties and interest on the basis that the workers met the tests for independent contractor status during the assessed years and that the assessments do not reflect the actual employment relationship.
