Issue: Reassessment under the Select Luxury Items Tax Act (SLITA) relating to the purchase of a used pleasure craft during the 2024 period. Relief sought: Vacate the reassessment or adjust it to reflect the actual consideration paid and applicable deductions; refund any overpaid SLITA with statutory interest; and waive related penalties.
Facts and reasons: On 2024-05-28 I purchased a 2019 30' motor yacht (HIN: CA-MLD9A37D919, engine S/N: V8-420-BC14273) via a licensed broker for CAD 246,000, as shown on the signed bill of sale and closing statement. The Canada Revenue Agency reassessed on 2025-02-10 using a taxable value of CAD 275,000 based on a generic pricing guide, increasing the SLITA assessed. The reassessment does not account for negotiated reductions due to documented engine hours (612 hrs), gelcoat blistering, electronics requiring replacement, and a pre-purchase marine survey dated 2024-06-05 identifying repairs estimated at CAD 18,500. In addition, a CAD 6,000 brokerage fee paid by me, not the seller, was incorrectly included in the taxable base. Evidence includes the broker's invoice, the wire transfer confirmation from 2024-05-28, the signed bill of sale, the marine survey with photos and repair estimates, insurance binder reflecting agreed value of CAD 246,000, and email correspondence confirming the price adjustments. I request that the taxable amount be corrected to the documented consideration, that any excess SLITA assessed be refunded with interest, and that penalties be cancelled as the reporting was made in good faith with complete documentation.
